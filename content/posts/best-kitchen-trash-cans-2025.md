## What to Look For in a Kitchen Trash Can

Here's what separates a good trash can from one you'll want to throw away.

### Capacity

Most kitchen trash cans come in 10, 13, or 20+ gallon sizes. The 13-gallon size is the sweet spot for most households: it fits standard 13-gallon kitchen bags (the ones you already have) and needs emptying every 2-3 days for a family of four. Ten-gallon cans are for small kitchens or single people — they fit under counters better but you'll empty them constantly. If you cook a lot or hate taking out the trash, go bigger, but remember: a 50-gallon can takes up floor space and holds a full week of trash that you have to smell in between.

### Lid Mechanism: Step vs. Touchless vs. Manual

This is the big decision. Step cans open with a foot pedal — reliable, no batteries, works every time, but the pedals are the #1 failure point on cheap cans. Touchless cans use a motion sensor to open the lid, which is great when your hands are covered in raw chicken juice, but they need batteries (or an AC adapter) and the sensors can be finicky — some open when you walk past, some don't open when you actually need them. Manual push lids are the cheapest but defeat the whole purpose if you're trying to avoid touching the can. If you have pets, look for a lock feature — the sensor opening for every dog that walks by is a real problem, and it's also how the dog gets into the trash.

### Odor Control

A sealed lid is the single biggest factor in kitchen odor. Step and touchless cans both seal when closed; open-top cans don't. Some premium cans add charcoal filters in the lid, which do help with odors from food scraps. If you compost, a sealed can with a filter makes a big difference. Also worth checking: does the lid actually seal? Some cans have a visible gap when closed, which defeats the whole purpose.

### Build Quality and Finish

Stainless steel looks great and hides nothing — every fingerprint shows. Brushed steel hides fingerprints much better than polished. Cheap steel dents, and cheap plastic cracks at the hinge. Look for a can with a removable inner bucket (so you can lift the whole bag out instead of wrestling it) and a soft-close lid that doesn't slam. The difference between a $40 can and a $100 can is mostly the hinge mechanism, the steel gauge, and the sensor quality — not the brand name.

## FAQ

### Are touchless trash cans worth it?

If you cook meat, handle raw chicken, or bake, yes. The whole point is not touching a lid covered in whatever was on your hands. They're also genuinely useful when your hands are full — a wave of the elbow opens the lid. But they cost more, need batteries, and the sensors can misbehave. If you're on a budget, a good step can gets you 90% of the benefit for half the price.

### What size trash can should I get for a family of four?

A 13-gallon can, which you'll empty every 2-3 days. That's the standard kitchen size and it fits the standard 13-gallon bags you already buy. If you generate a lot of food waste or only want to take the trash out weekly, step up to 20+ gallons, but be ready for the floor space it takes.

### Why does my trash can smell even with a lid?

Three reasons: the lid doesn't seal completely (visible gap when closed), you're not using a sealed bag, or odors are coming from around the bag rim. A sealed lid with a charcoal filter is the fix for most kitchens. Also: rinse food containers before tossing them, and take out food waste more often than the rest of the trash.

### Can I use regular trash bags in a sensor can?

Most sensor cans fit standard 13-gallon bags. The exceptions are simplehuman cans, which are designed around their proprietary liner system — standard bags work but fit loosely. Check the product description for "fits standard bags" before buying, and avoid cans that force you into a specific bag brand.

### Do sensor cans work with pets around?

Some do, some don't. Motion sensors can't tell a dog from a hand, so a curious pet walking past will trigger the lid. If you have pets, look for a can with a pet lock (the iTouchless AbsorbX has one) or a sensor with an adjustable range you can tune down. Also consider the lid-lock feature so a determined pet can't nose the lid open.

### How long do sensor can batteries last?

Most sensor cans run 6-12 months on a set of batteries with normal use (opening 10-20 times a day). Some models support AC adapters, which is worth it if you never want to think about batteries. When the lid starts opening slowly or not at all, it's almost always the batteries, not the sensor.
