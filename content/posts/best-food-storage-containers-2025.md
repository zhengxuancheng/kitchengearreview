## What to Look For

### Glass vs. Plastic

Glass containers are oven-safe, microwave-safe, and never stain — but they're heavy and breakable. BPA-free Tritan plastic is lightweight and nearly unbreakable but can stain from tomato sauce and may warp in the microwave over time. Your choice depends on how you use them: glass for reheating leftovers, plastic for packing lunches.

### Leak-Proof Lids

The single most important feature. A "leak-proof" claim means nothing if the lid pops open in your lunch bag. Look for containers with four locking tabs (not two), a silicone gasket seal, and lids that audibly click when properly closed. The best sets survive a shake test with soup inside.

### Stackability and Storage

A good set nests together when empty — bowls inside larger bowls, lids in a separate organizer. Sets that don't nest turn your cabinet into Jenga. Look for square or rectangular shapes rather than round — they use shelf space more efficiently.

## FAQ

### Is glass really better than plastic?

For reheating — absolutely. Glass won't leach chemicals, won't stain, and won't hold odors. For packing lunches in a bag — plastic wins on weight and durability. The best kitchen has both.

### Why do my plastic containers get stained?

Tomato sauce, turmeric, and curry contain oil-soluble pigments that bind to plastic. Glass is non-porous and never stains. Switching to glass for saucy foods is the only permanent solution. For plastic, rub with a paste of baking soda and water after washing.

### Can I freeze glass containers?

Yes, but leave headspace — liquids expand when frozen. Tempered glass handles freezing well, but avoid thermal shock: don't go directly from freezer to hot oven. Let it thaw in the fridge overnight or use the microwave defrost setting.
