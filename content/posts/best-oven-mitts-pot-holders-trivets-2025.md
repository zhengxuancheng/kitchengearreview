Burned wrists and dropped casseroles are the two most common kitchen injuries involving hot cookware. A good oven mitt stops both. A bad one either transfers heat through the fabric or makes your hands clumsy enough to drop things. We tested six kitchen heat-protection products — silicone mitts, traditional quilted cotton, glove-style oven mitts, pot holders, and trivets — to find which ones actually protect your hands.

Each product had to hold a 425°F sheet pan for 30 seconds, grip a cast iron skillet handle, pick up a glass baking dish, and set hot cookware down on trivets. The results were surprising.

## What to Look For

### Heat Resistance and Material

The material determines the safe temperature ceiling. Silicone mitts are rated to 450–500°F — enough for most oven use but not for broiler grates or direct flame contact. Quilted cotton or aramid fiber (Nomex/Kevlar) mitts handle higher temperatures, often 500–650°F, and have the advantage of not conducting heat through the material the way silicone does when the mitt gets wet or greasy. Look for a mitt with multiple layers — a single layer of silicone or cotton offers minimal protection against sustained contact with a hot surface. The best mitts combine an outer heat barrier, an inner insulating layer, and a moisture-wicking inner lining.

### Grip and Dexterity

A mitt that you cannot grip with is dangerous. Silicone nubs or textured rubber provide excellent wet grip but can feel sticky against dry glass. Cotton and aramid fabric mitts let you adjust your grip more easily but pick up grease and lose grip over time. Glove-style mitts give you finger dexterity — you can actually tie an apron string or grab a small jar from a hot oven — at the cost of less overall heat protection than a full mitt that covers the whole hand. Clip-on pot holders sacrifice dexterity entirely but handle the specific job of gripping hot handles reliably.

### Length and Coverage

Standard oven mitts cover the hand and about 2–3 inches of wrist. Quarter-length (8–10 inch) mitts cover the lower forearm. If you regularly reach into a deep oven to pull out a roasting pan on the bottom rack, go with 14+ inch mitts. Burns happen when the mitt shifts down and exposes bare skin to the oven rack or the side of a hot pan. The cuff should be wide enough to let you pull your hand out quickly, but snug enough that the mitt does not slide down when you lift a heavy pot.

### Trivet Material and Surface Safety

Trivets protect your countertops from hot cookware. Cork trivets are natural, non-slip, and heat-resistant up to 400°F but stain easily and wear out within a year. Silicone trivets handle up to 500°F, are dishwasher-safe, and grip smooth surfaces well — but they can leave heat marks on delicate quartz countertops if the cookware is extremely hot. Metal trivets last forever and look great but need felt or silicone feet to prevent scratching. Wood trivets are beautiful insulators but can crack from repeated heat exposure.

## FAQ

### Can I use silicone mitts under the broiler?

Silicone mitts are typically safe to 450–500°F, but direct flame or contact with a broiler element can exceed that temperature. Use aramid or quilted cotton mitts for broiler work, or keep the silicone mitt dry and avoid direct contact with the heating element.

### How often should I replace oven mitts?

Replace quilted cotton mitts when the insulation feels compressed or you start feeling heat through the fabric — usually 1–2 years with regular use. Silicone mitts last longer (2–4 years) but should be replaced if the silicone cracks, melts, or loses its textured grip.

### What is the safest material for kitchen trivets?

Cork and silicone are the safest for standard countertops. Cork is naturally non-slip and self-extinguishing but stains easily. Silicone is the best all-around material — heat resistant to 500°F, dishwasher safe, and non-slip on both the counter and the cookware. Avoid thin metal trivets without rubber feet — they can scratch quartz and granite surfaces.

### Are pot holders safer than oven mitts?

For gripping handles and lids, pot holders are safer because you have more direct tactile feedback from the object. But for reaching into an oven or handling large dishes, mitts or gloves protect more of your hand and forearm. The right choice depends on the task — experienced cooks keep both.

### Can I wash silicone oven mitts in the dishwasher?

Most silicone mitts are dishwasher-safe, but check the manufacturer's instructions. The cotton lining in hybrid mitts may not be dishwasher-safe. Standard silicone-only mitts and trivets clean up perfectly in the top rack of the dishwasher.
