## What to Look For in Baking Pans

### Material

Aluminum is the standard for even heating. It conducts heat well and doesn't have hot spots, which means your cakes bake evenly and your muffins come out the same color. The downside is that bare aluminum reacts with acidic ingredients like tomato sauce or citrus — you'll get a metallic taste. Anodized aluminum solves this with a hard, non-reactive surface.

Stainless steel looks good and resists corrosion, but it's not as conductive as aluminum. Good stainless steel pans have an aluminum or copper core sandwiched between layers. Cheap stainless steel pans have hot spots that burn edges while the center stays underdone.

Carbon steel heats like cast iron but weighs less. It needs seasoning, and it will rust if you leave it wet. For baking, it's excellent for deep-dish pizzas and cornbread but overkill for standard cakes.

Silicone is flexible and non-stick by nature. The problem is structural support — a silicone pan full of batter is floppy and hard to move without spilling. You need to put it on a metal baking sheet first. It also doesn't brown crusts the way metal does.

### Non-Stick Coating

Non-stick makes cleanup easy, but the coating has a lifespan. Good non-stick lasts 2-3 years with careful use. Cheap non-stick flakes within months. The trade-off is that non-stick pans can't go in the dishwasher, can't be used with metal utensils, and shouldn't be stacked without protective layers. For bakers who make things that stick (brownies, sticky buns), non-stick is worth it. For standard cakes and muffins, a well-greased aluminum pan performs just as well.

### Weight and Gauge

Thicker pans cost more and perform better. A lightweight pan warps in the oven, heats unevenly, and dents when you drop it in the sink. A heavy-gauge pan stays flat, distributes heat evenly, and lasts for years. For aluminum pans, look for 0.8mm thickness or more. For carbon steel, 1mm or more. If a pan feels flimsy in your hand, it won't get better in the oven.

### Shape and Depth

Standard round cake pans are 8 or 9 inches wide and 2 inches deep. If you're making layer cakes, get 2-inch deep pans — 1.5-inch pans don't leave enough room for the cake to rise. Loaf pans come in 8x4 and 9x5 sizes, and one batch of batter won't always fit both. Muffin tins typically have 12 cavities, and the standard size is about 2.75 inches across and 1.25 inches deep. Mini muffin tins are about half that.

## FAQ

### Aluminum vs. non-stick: which is better for cake pans?

It depends on how much you hate scrubbing. Aluminum gives you the best heat conduction and even baking, but you need to grease and flour the pan, and some cakes still stick. Non-stick releases more easily but the coating degrades and eventually needs replacing. For frequent bakers who make layer cakes, I recommend aluminum pans (Fat Daddio's). For occasional bakers who want the easiest release, go with a quality non-stick like Williams Sonoma Goldtouch.

### Can I use metal utensils on non-stick bakeware?

No. Metal utensils scratch non-stick coatings and accelerate flaking. Use silicone, nylon, or wood utensils with non-stick bakeware. If you prefer metal for cutting brownies in the pan, use an uncoated aluminum or stainless steel pan instead.

### Why do my cake layers dome in the center?

Domed cake layers are usually caused by oven temperature being too high, which sets the edges before the center finishes rising, or by over-mixing the batter, which develops too much gluten. To fix it, lower your oven temperature by 25°F, use aluminum pans (they heat more evenly), and wrap the outside of the pans with a damp cake strip to insulate the edges. Or just level the domes with a serrated knife — most layer cakes get trimmed anyway.

### How do I keep muffins from sticking without paper liners?

Grease the cavities thoroughly with butter or shortening, making sure to get into the corners at the bottom. Dust with flour and tap out the excess. For even better results, use a baking spray that contains flour (like Baker's Joy). A quality non-stick pan like the Wilton Recipe Right also helps significantly. Let muffins cool in the pan for 5 minutes before trying to remove them — trying too early tears the bottoms.

### What size loaf pan should I use for standard bread recipes?

Standard loaf recipes are written for a 9x5-inch pan. An 8x4-inch pan will work but the loaf will overflow as it rises. If you're not sure which size you have, measure the inside dimensions at the top rim. Most banana bread and pound cake recipes produce about 4-5 cups of batter, which fits a 9x5 pan with about an inch of headroom.
