Countertop composting is having a moment in 2025. The reason is simple: more people want to cut down on food waste, but nobody wants a slimy bucket of scraps under the sink attracting fruit flies.

They come in two styles. **Electric composters** use heat, a grinding blade, and a carbon filter to dehydrate and deodorize scraps in 4–8 hours. A week of peels, eggshells, and coffee grounds comes out as a dry powder. **Manual compost bins** rely on a charcoal filter and a tight lid to keep scraps smelling fine until you carry them to the garden or the municipal compost drop-off.

We tested six of the best — from a $100 Lomi competitor to a $30 countertop bin that costs less than a bag of potting soil.

## FAQ

### What's the difference between electric composters and traditional composting?

Electric composters dehydrate and grind scraps into a dry powder. The result isn't true compost — it hasn't broken down through microbial activity. You can mix it into garden soil where it finishes decomposing, or use it as a soil amendment. Traditional composting (hot pile, worm bin, bokashi) takes weeks to months but produces richer, living compost. Electric is fast and clean. Traditional is slower but more sustainable.

### Do countertop composters smell?

Electric models with carbon filters shouldn't smell during operation — the filter absorbs odors before they escape. Manual bins rely on charcoal filtration and a tight lid seal. Both work well if you keep up with filter replacements. Don't let scraps sit for more than a week in a manual bin in warm weather.

### Can I compost meat and dairy?

Electric models vary. Lomi recommends against meat and dairy in Grow mode but allows them in Eco mode. BeyondGREEN handles meat and dairy in any mode. Manual bins can store meat and dairy just fine — just make sure you take them to an outdoor pile or municipal compost within a couple of days, because they'll start rotting faster than vegetable scraps.

### How often do I need to replace the filters?

Electric composter filters typically last 3–6 months depending on usage. Manual bin charcoal filters need replacement every 2–3 months. If you start smelling your scraps before opening the bin, it's time. Some brands (OXO, Chef'n) sell filter packs, and generic charcoal sheets can be cut to size for many models.
